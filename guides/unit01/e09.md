Management has assigned you, an electronics engineer, to work with a software engineer and recreate the classic game of Simon.

In Simon, the game lights up red, green, blue, and yellow lights in an order that the player needs to follow. Each light has an associated sound to help the player remember the order.

Every time the player successfully follows the order, the game adds another light to the sequence. Players compete to see how many lights they can remember and repeat.

# Your hardware engineering job

It is your job to design and build a prototype Simon game using an Arduino Uno, a breadboard, and electronic components. Your game must contain at least the following components:

Four LEDs with the colors red, green, blue, and yellow and 1000Ω (1k) resistors to protect the LED.

Four switches the player presses to follow the pattern. Use the same pull-down model for the switches that we’ve used in previous projects.

A speaker with a 1000Ω (1k) resistor to play the sounds.

An LCD display to show score and instructions.

## Software development

You have an AI software engineering partner who will take your specifications and create Arduino Software that will play the game.

The software engineer will ask you the pin numbers for your LEDs and your switches. It will also ask you if you know where the SDA and SCL pins are on your Arduino. **SDA should be connected to A4 and SCL should be connected to A5.**

## Design and simulate

Before you build your Simon game, you will design it in Tinkercad and test your software in the simulator.

Tinkercad has 16x2 LCD display you can use in your simulation. As you did with the LCD “Hello, world” project, you will use the PCF8574-based component at address 0x27:

### Simulation Checklist

Before you attempt to run the full Simon Game, follow this checklist in Tinkercad to ensure your hardware is wired correctly. A single misplaced wire can make the software appear broken even when the code is perfect!

**Resistor Verification:** Confirm you have used 1000Ω resistors for all four LEDs, all four switches (pull-down configuration), and the speaker.

**I2C LCD Wiring:** * Connect **GND** to the negative rail and **VCC** to the positive rail.

Connect **SDA** to pin **A4** and **SCL** to pin **A5**.

**Power the Rails:** Ensure you have jumped the Arduino’s **5V** pin to the breadboard’s positive (+) rail and the **GND** pin to the negative (-) rail.

**Hardware Test (Crucial Step):** Before asking for the full game code, ask your AI partner to:*"Generate a simple hardware test script. When I press a button, its corresponding LED should light up and the speaker should play a tone. It should also display 'Testing...' on the LCD."*

**Pin Mapping:** Make sure the pin numbers you used in your Tinkercad circuit match exactly what you tell your AI Software Engineer.
